# The offer — Local Visibility

So any agent describes it correctly in a note a human will read on a call.

## What is being sold

**Local Visibility — $400 setup, $200/month.**

Getting a small contractor's Google Business Profile built properly, and then
keeping it that way.

### Setup ($400, roughly 2–4 hours)
- Claim and verify the profile if it is unclaimed
- Fix the primary category, add real secondary categories
- Link the website
- Services listed individually with descriptions
- Business description, all 750 characters
- Service area, hours including holidays
- 20+ photos, sourced from the client
- Seed and answer the Q&A section
- LocalBusiness schema markup on the website
- Stand up a compliant review request system

### Monthly ($200)
- Review monitoring, responses drafted
- Two posts a month
- Photo refresh
- One-page report: calls from the profile, review count, position
- Policy watch

The monthly is not padding. Reviews arrive and need answering, profiles decay,
and Google changed its review policy twice in 2026 without announcing either
change. Somebody has to be watching.

## Website work

A **sequenced second step**, never an upsell.

It comes up only when the audit shows the site is undermining the profile —
not linked, no schema, NAP mismatch, or too slow to hold the click. That is an
if-then a human can say out loud without flinching. "Get the small one so I can
pitch the big one" is not, and a contractor will hear it.

| Tier | Price | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| Fix-up | $750–1,500 | Their site stays; fix what the audit found |
| Template rebuild | $2,000–2,800 | New site on a vertical template |
| Custom | $3,500–5,000 | Designed specifically for them |

First three clients are discounted **for a stated reason** — building case
studies, in exchange for a testimonial, use of their name, and two
introductions. Never a silent discount.

## Why the profile first

A homeowner with a dead furnace opens Maps, scans the top three, checks reviews
and photos, and calls. Most never reach a website. The profile is the
storefront; the site is the second look.

The map pack also ranks on things that can actually be fixed — categories,
services, photos, review activity. Organic search ranking is a fight over
domain authority that cannot be won for a contractor in a sensible timeframe.

## The opener

**The unlinked website.** "Did you know your website isn't linked on your
Google listing?" gets a genuine reaction, and the fix takes two minutes — which
means value can be demonstrated before anyone has paid anything.

Second best: a wrong primary category. An HVAC company filed under
"Contractor" is invisible for the search that matters.

**The first call does not mention price.** The goal is permission to send the
free Local Visibility Report. The report — specifically its competitor
comparison table — does the selling. Price comes up when they call back.

## Voice

- Plain and short. These are working people taking a call from a truck.
- Name the finding, not the service. "Your listing doesn't have your website on
  it" — not "I do local SEO optimization."
- Never say: SEO, optimization, digital marketing, funnel, leads, platform,
  solution, presence, brand.
- Say: your Google listing, your Maps listing, photos, reviews, categories.
- Never claim a ranking outcome. No "I'll get you to number one."
- Never disparage whoever built their current site. It is often the owner's
  nephew.

## Reviews — the compliance line

Google rewrote this policy on 16–17 April 2026 with no announcement, and
enforcement is automated. In the US the FTC's 2024 rule adds legal exposure on
top of platform penalties.

**Never propose, and flag as risk if seen:** incentives of any kind, review
gating, asking on the premises, kiosks or shared devices, staff quotas, asking
a customer to name an employee or include specific content, reviews from staff
or family.

**The compliant system:** ask every customer equally, after they have left, on
their own device, using the profile's short link, completely open-ended, two to
three touches over 7–10 days, respond to everything.

If a prospect's reviews look purchased — duplicates, generic text, off-topic
content — the finding is written as *the reviews don't describe the actual
work, which reads generic to customers and as a manipulation signal to Google.*
**Never "your reviews are fake."** The owner most likely bought a package from
a vendor and had no idea.
